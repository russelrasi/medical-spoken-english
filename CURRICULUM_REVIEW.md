# Month 1 curriculum review (coach's view)

Scope: Classes 1 to 12 as they exist in `public/course.json`. Learner: Samira, a first-year student whose first language is Bangla and who will study medicine in English. Written as recommendations only; nothing in the course data has been changed.

## What works well

- Sensible grammar spine: be, have, present simple, present continuous, questions, past, future, requests, description. This is the right core for survival English.
- Every class ends in speaking practice and a short dialogue, so grammar is tied to use.
- Bangla support is used for explanation and contrast (for example verb position, he/she), which is exactly where a Bangla speaker needs it.
- Revision at the end of the month, with a test.

## Gaps and risks (most important first)

1. **No pronunciation or listening foundation.** Bangla speakers typically need explicit work on: /v/ and /w/ ("very" and "wery"), /θ/ and /ð/ ("think", "this"), /z/ versus /dʒ/, word-final consonants and clusters, schwa and weak forms, word stress, and the rise and fall of intonation. Without this, accurate grammar will still be hard to understand in a lecture hall or ward. Class 1 audio is the only model she hears, in one voice accent at a time.
   - Add a 10 minute "Sound of the week" slot in every class, and one dedicated Pronunciation class in Week 1 (alphabet and spelling aloud, syllable stress, the four or five Bangla-specific problem sounds).
2. **Medical English starts too late (or not at all in Month 1).** The vocabulary is general (hobby, family, market). She will meet "anatomy", "lecture", "dissection" in week one of college. Fix: keep general English as the spine but add a "Medical corner" of 5 to 6 words per class: body parts and systems (Class 3 to 4), anatomy and hospital places (Class 6), symptoms and "Where does it hurt?" (Class 10 to 11), instructions a professor gives ("Open your books", "Label the diagram").
3. **Too much grammar per class for a beginner.** Class 3 packs present simple, the -s ending and do/does; Class 4 contrasts simple versus continuous; Class 8 asks for twenty irregular verbs. Recommend: one new idea per class, with the second half used for recycling. Teach Class 1 and 2 more slowly (see 5) and move "present simple versus continuous" to a revision slot.
4. **Not enough built-in recycling.** There is no spaced review of earlier classes inside later lessons. Add a "Warm-up from last class" (5 questions drawn from earlier lessons) and a vocabulary box that re-surfaces words after 1, 3 and 7 days.
5. **A real English "starter pack" is missing before Class 1.** Suggest a Class 0 (or the first half of Class 1): the alphabet and spelling her name, numbers and dates, time, days and months, classroom language ("Could you repeat that?"), greetings at different levels of formality. Many Class 1 tasks assume this.
6. **Listening is thin.** Listening exercises are single sentences in one accent. Add short two-speaker dialogues, different speeds and accents (the app already offers US, UK and Indian English voices; use them on purpose), and "listen for the number or time" tasks. Her future lectures will be in fast, accented English.
7. **Production is manual and unscored.** Speaking practice is send-a-voice-note. Add a quick self-check checklist per recording (Did I use the verb? Did I say the -s? Did I stress the right word?) and have the teacher return one short comment. If you want automatic feedback later, the browser's speech recognition can score read-aloud accuracy.
8. **Writing is missing.** Add one very short writing task per week (3 to 5 sentences: introduce yourself, describe your hostel, a message to a lecturer) with a model answer. Writing consolidates grammar in a way multiple choice cannot.
9. **Register and politeness need earlier attention.** Polite requests arrive in Class 10. Move "Could you / May I / Excuse me" to Class 2 or 3, because she needs them from day one with professors.
10. **Assessment is only at the end.** Add a 10 minute check at the end of each week (Classes 3, 6, 9) so gaps are found in week 1, not week 4.

## Suggested resequencing (same 12 classes, small changes)

| Class | Current | Suggested adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Self-introduction, to be | Add alphabet, numbers, spelling name; keep to be only |
| 2 | Family, have/has, possessives | Add "Could you repeat that?" and 5 classroom phrases |
| 3 | Daily routine, present simple | Split: routine only; move do/does to Class 4 |
| 4 | Present continuous | Replace with do/does and negatives; continuous becomes Class 5 |
| 5 | Likes, hobbies | Present continuous now; keep like + verb-ing as a short add-on |
| 6 | WH-questions, time prepositions | Keep; add medical places (ward, lab, library) |
| 7 | Past simple was/were, -ed | Keep; add pronunciation of -ed (three sounds) |
| 8 | Irregular verbs | Limit to 10 verbs; recycle 10 more in Month 2 |
| 9 | Future | Keep; add "Plan your week" writing |
| 10 | Polite requests | Keep; add patient-facing phrases (basic) |
| 11 | Description, there is/are | Keep; add describing symptoms in simple terms |
| 12 | Revision and test | Add a speaking test and a listening test, not only written |

## How to use the app better for this learner

- Use the new **Mistakes to review** and **Took a while** sections after each lesson as the teacher's checklist: it shows where the student hesitates, which is often a better sign of weakness than a wrong answer.
- Use the 100 question **homework** as the daily 15 minute drill, and the **explainer** as the pre-lesson viewing.
- Keep one voice for a week, then switch accents deliberately.
